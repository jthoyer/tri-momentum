02

Type

One family, Outfit, doing every job — this replaced the earlier DM Sans (body/display) + DM Mono (labels) pairing. With only one typeface, the sentence-you-read vs. the-interface-talking-about-itself distinction is now carried by weight, case, and letter-spacing instead of a family switch: body and titles stay sentence case at regular tracking, while counters/eyebrows/timestamps/badges go uppercase with wide tracking, the way the "Mono label" row below still reads distinctly even though it's the same typeface as everything above it.

Add a race toggle--blue pill · flips to solid-white "Cancel" when open

Sits above the add-race form on the Races tab, which is collapsed by default. Tapping it reveals the form and the button itself flips to "Cancel" — a solid white (--s1) fill with a --bd2 border, not transparent. A transparent fill on the page's --bg background read as a disabled/unpressable button in testing; white + border reads as a real, tappable secondary button. The form auto-collapses back behind this button the moment a race is successfully added. Mirrors the toggle-a-hidden-form pattern from the athlete's club calendar reference app's "Add a race +" button, adapted to this app's own button language (and its own "+"-free label) rather than copying its colours.

05

Voice

Tip copy is the product's actual IP, so the tone rules are as fixed as the colour tokens. Five rules, then the pattern they produce.

01
Mechanism first

Every tip explains why before what. Athletes who understand the mechanism self-correct under fatigue; athletes who only get the instruction forget it.

02
No hand-holding

The reader has strong self-knowledge and will push back on weak reasoning. Write for that reader, not a beginner.

03
Specificity over generality

"Drop 10 BPM and observe how Monday feels" beats "train in Zone 2." Name the number, the session, the observation.

04
Sharp vs mediocre, not good vs bad

The contrast pair is never a strawman. The mediocre response is defensible — what a reasonable athlete does without thinking carefully. That's what makes the sharp response worth reading.

05
Phase-aware, not calendar-aware

"Base Wk1 Foundations" is about fresh legs and an established floor — regardless of what month it is. Content never assumes a specific calendar date.

Rule 4, applied

Sharp

"Finished 8 BPM over the ceiling you set. That's not a fail — it's information: either the ceiling was wrong, or the pace felt more controlled than your heart rate says it was. Which one was it?"

Mediocre

"Try to stay closer to your target heart rate next time — going too hard on easy days can affect your recovery."
