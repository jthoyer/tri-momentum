First thing shown on load, base nav visible beneath it throughout. Dismissed only by tapping a nav tab — no auto-advance timer, no separate "get started" control. Headline is the page-title spec (23px / 600 / −0.3px, line-height 1.25) in white, directly under the logo — no separate uppercase sub head above it any more (dropped in favour of the headline speaking for itself). No drop shadow on the logo — flat against the fill. Below the headline, with double the original gap (56px, up from 28px, so it reads as a distinct block rather than crowding the headline), a translucent countdown card names the nearest upcoming committed race from the Races tab ("Locked in," not "Considering") and how far out it is — hidden entirely, not shown empty, when there's no upcoming locked race. Below it, a second card in the identical treatment shows what's on today from the training plan: one tappable row per planned session (each row its own button — with two sessions planned, one card-level tap would have to guess which was just finished), or the words "Rest day" when the week has a plan but today isn't in it. A session that has already been logged is shown, but as a static row marked "Logged" rather than a tappable one — a <div>, not a disabled button, so there is nothing to hunt for that would re-enable it; still-to-log rows come first. Offering it as an equal tap target is not a cosmetic repeat: it writes a duplicate entry that Adherence then reports as an unplanned extra. Unlike the Check-in landing, an all-logged day still renders the card — this surface is informational as well as an action prompt. When nothing at all is planned for the current week the card is removed from the DOM, not merely hidden as the countdown is — an athlete who has never entered a plan should find no trace of the feature. Both cards are width:100% capped at max-width, not shrink-to-fit, so they align rather than stacking at two different widths. A fourth, independent section, "Earlier this week," appends below Today's own state — list, Rest day, or nothing — separated by the same top-border divider Today already uses between its own rows. It lists still-open sessions from earlier days in the current week, each with a small uppercase day label ("MON 17") above the usual name/meta lines, tapping into the identical check-in flow a Today row uses but dated from that session's own day rather than assumed to be today. Deliberately independent of Today's own state — a rest day today says nothing about whether Monday's session ever got logged — and, like the logged Today row, drops off the list the moment its session is logged, on this device or another.

Label text on all three cards is rgba(255,255,255,.82), not the original .7/.75 — measured against the card's composited background those read 3.97:1 and 4.35:1, under the 4.5:1 AA bar; .82 clears at 4.93:1. The Today card's buttons also override the global --blue focus ring to white — a blue ring on a blue background is no ring at all.
Fill is the canonical --blue token (#0022FF) — same colour as the favicon artwork itself, so the logo sits flush with no edge. Still not the base nav's hardcoded #002DFF (see below); that discrepancy is separate and unrelated.

The same cell as above, but driven by the plan rather than the log — so it can draw a segment for a session that was planned and never logged, which the grid had no way to represent before. Three states join the shared good/mid/bad fills. missed (planned, nothing logged) is a 1.5px inset --dash-bad ring over a 135° hatch of the same colour at 42%. planned (“not due yet” — the day hasn’t passed, so it is not a miss) is a 1px inset --ink3 ring on transparent. extra (logged with no matching plan row) is a solid --ink3 fill — grey, never red, because an unplanned session is not a failure. The missed/bad split is deliberate: hatched means no logged entry at all; solid --dash-bad means logged and reported as not done. Different facts, different treatment. No new colour token was introduced — both existing tokens already carried the right semantics, so there is nothing new in the palette above. Contrast measured, not estimated: the hatch is decorative and carries none of the burden, while the solid rings clear the 3:1 non-text bar comfortably — --dash-bad 5.74:1 on --s1 / 4.82:1 on --s4, --ink3 5.25:1 / 4.41:1. The legend is signal-aware and grows from five items to seven here; each swatch is styled with the actual grid modifier class it describes, so it can never drift from what it captions. At 320px the seven items wrap to three rows with no horizontal overflow. .dash-cell-seg.missed/.planned/.extra and dashLegend() in index.html.

Sits above the add-race form on the Races tab, which is collapsed by default (.race-add-toggle, --blue fill, full width). Mirrors the toggle-a-hidden-form pattern from the athlete's club calendar reference app's "Add a race +" button, adapted to this app's own button language (and its own "+"-free label) rather than copying its colours.

The Plan view is where the athlete types in the week they've already written elsewhere — the app never authors or suggests a session, it only stores what was planned so it has something objective to measure the logged week against. Deliberately built out of the Races component language rather than a second one: the session card is the same native <details> disclosure and the same soft two-layer "lifted card" shadow as a race card, just at --rmd (10px) instead of --rlg. The always-visible summary carries a discipline dot filled straight from the --{disc} token (so it matches the "by session" grid and the rest of the app), the title, an uppercase meta line, and a Key pill on key sessions (--phase-peak on --phase-peak-l, 6.03:1 — same reversed-fill convention as the Month week phase pill). Notes, Edit and Remove sit behind the expand — the foot is space-between, not flex-end, so the safe action and the destructive one sit at opposite ends rather than adjacent, where a mis-tap deletes a session the athlete meant to edit. Edit opens the same add-session form pre-filled, relabelled "Save changes": every field is editable except the date, because moving a session to another day is the drag-and-drop calendar editing this feature has always excluded — delete-and-re-add remains the way to change a date. Week selector: an --s3 track with 44px ‹/› buttons, reusing the Month week number, phase pill and "this week" tag verbatim — the Plan view has no logged sessions to infer a phase from, so its pill reads the app's single current phase, the same way This week's own header does. Add button: full-width and dashed, in --blue-m rather than --bd3 — --bd3 over the page background only reaches 1.73:1, under the 3:1 non-text bar in Accessibility below, while --blue-m clears it at 3.71:1 and ties the outline to the blue label inside it. Reached from an "Edit plan →" link in This week, not a sixth nav tab — the nav stays five buttons, and Month had already established that a full view can live without one. Synced to the same Google Sheet as the training log and the race calendar, via a third "Plan" tab in the spreadsheet.

Check-in is the app's primary interaction, and when today has a planned session the honest opening question is "did you do the one you planned?", not a blank date-and-discipline form. This screen is that default; the generic form becomes a deliberate choice. It is a fourth mode of the existing check-in state machine (ask | plan | review | saved) rather than a separate view, so one render() still decides what the flow looks like — which means every branch that previously treated its else as "ask" needed an explicit plan case, or the landing would draw under a "Just finished · 1 / 5" progress header. Chrome: eyebrow "Today", no step counter and a 0% bar — the landing sits before step 1, so any counter would misstate where the athlete is — and the sticky Back/Next pair hidden outright rather than disabled, since a greyed "Next" on a screen with no next step reads as breakage. Cards are the same flowPlanCard() component as the in-flow hint list, at raised weight (--blue-m border, more padding) because here they are the primary action rather than a shortcut above the real question; only the call-to-action wording differs. Each session is its own card with its own action — with two planned, a single shared action would have to guess which, and guessing wrong pre-fills the wrong discipline. "Log an unplanned session" is a named design element, not an oversight: training off-plan is normal, and the signal layer already models it as extra, never scoring it bad. It drops into the manual flow exactly as it behaved before any plan integration, with nothing pre-filled. Two rules carry over unchanged: a1 is never pre-filled by any path, and the landing only appears when nothing has been entered yet — tapping away to Review and back still resumes an in-progress check-in rather than discarding it. A day with nothing planned never sees this screen at all. A session already logged today is shown but not offered (second card above): muted --s3 fill, no lift, no hover, and a --done "Logged" marker (5.54:1) where the call-to-action would be. It is rendered as a plain <div> rather than a disabled button, because there is no action to re-enable and a disabled control invites a hunt for the thing that would turn it back on. Which sessions count as logged comes from planDayResults() — the same matcher the Adherence signal runs on, never a second rule — so the screen and the signal can't disagree. This is not cosmetic: tapping a card for a session already logged writes a duplicate entry, which the matcher then reports as an unplanned extra, corrupting the signal this screen exists to feed. When every planned session for today is already logged the screen doesn't appear at all, and Check-in falls through to the manual flow.

All seven days render in order whether or not anything is planned, so a week with an empty plan is still seven rows each offering Add — the shortest path from "never used this" to a first entered session. 05

Accessibility

A standing check, not a one-off audit — two tokens have already shipped, failed, and been caught here. Both times the fix was made at the token level, not with a per-component override, so every screen using that token got corrected at once.

Text must clear WCAG AA contrast against its actual background — 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text (18px+ regular, or 14px+/18.66px+ bold) and UI components/icons. Check a new colour against where it's actually used, not against --bg or white in isolation — a chip on a tinted fill needs its own check.

06

Voice

Tip copy is the product's actual IP, so the tone rules are as fixed as the colour tokens. Five rules, then the pattern they produce.

01
Mechanism first

Every tip explains why before what. Athletes who understand the mechanism self-correct under fatigue; athletes who only get the instruction forget it.

02
No hand-holding

The reader has strong self-knowledge and will push back on weak reasoning. Write for that reader, not a beginner.

03
Specificity over generality

"Drop 10 BPM and observe how Monday feels" beats "train in Zone 2." Name the number, the session, the observation.

04
Sharp vs mediocre, not good vs bad

The contrast pair is never a strawman. The mediocre response is defensible — what a reasonable athlete does without thinking carefully. That's what makes the sharp response worth reading.

05
Phase-aware, not calendar-aware

"Base Wk1 Foundations" is about fresh legs and an established floor — regardless of what month it is. Content never assumes a specific calendar date.

Rule 4, applied

Sharp

"Finished 8 BPM over the ceiling you set. That's not a fail — it's information: either the ceiling was wrong, or the pace felt more controlled than your heart rate says it was. Which one was it?"

Mediocre

"Try to stay closer to your target heart rate next time — going too hard on easy days can affect your recovery."
